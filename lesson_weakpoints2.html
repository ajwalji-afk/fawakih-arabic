نِقَاطُ الضَّعْفِ
7 concepts from today's midterm — train until they stick
الإِضَافَة (Iḍāfah) — a possessive/genitive construction of two nouns. The first noun is the مُضَاف (annexed noun — it loses tanwīn and al-). The second noun is the مُضَافٌ إِلَيْهِ (annexed-to noun — always مَجْرُور). Together they mean "the X of Y" or "Y's X."

كِتَابُ اللهِ · بَيْتُ الرَّجُلِ · رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ The مُضَاف never takes tanwīn and never takes ال. The مُضَافٌ إِلَيْهِ is always in the genitive (مَجْرُور).

The Sandwiched ن Rule: When a dual (مُثَنَّى) or masculine sound plural (جَمْعُ مُذَكَّرٍ سَالِم) is used as the مُضَاف in an إِضَافَة, its ن is dropped.

رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ ← عَالَمُونَ loses ن → عَالَمِي Dual: كِتَابَانِ → كِتَابَا اللهِ  |  Masc. pl: مُسْلِمُونَ → مُسْلِمُو الْبَلَدِ
The ن is "sandwiched" between the مُضَاف and مُضَافٌ إِلَيْهِ — so it is dropped.

Non-Human Plural Adjective Agreement: In Arabic, non-human plurals (animals, objects, abstract things) are treated grammatically as feminine singular. Any adjective describing them must also be feminine singular.

الْكُتُبُ الْكَبِيرَةُ · الْجِبَالُ الشَّامِخَةُ · الأَيَّامُ الطَّوِيلَةُ This rule applies even if the noun looks masculine or is grammatically plural — if it's a non-human plural, the adjective becomes feminine singular (ending ـَةٌ / ـَةُ / ـَةَ / ـَةِ).

Arabic nouns and adjectives have 4 grammatical states (الأَحْوَالُ الإِعْرَابِيَّة):
مَرْفُوع — nominative (default; subject, مُبْتَدَأ, خَبَر, فَاعِل) · ضَمَّة (ُ)
مَنْصُوب — accusative (مَفْعُول بِهِ, objects of إِنَّ, after كَانَ) · فَتْحَة (َ)
مَجْرُور — genitive (after prepositions, مُضَافٌ إِلَيْهِ) · كَسْرَة (ِ)
مَجْزُوم — jussive (verbs only: after لَمْ، لِـ، لَا النَّاهِيَة) · سُكُون (ْ)

فَاعِل vs مَفْعُولٌ بِهِ — the two main noun roles in a verbal sentence (جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة):

فَاعِل (doer/agent) — comes right after the verb, مَرْفُوع (ضَمَّة ُ), answers "who did it?"
مَفْعُولٌ بِهِ (direct object) — comes after the فَاعِل, مَنْصُوب (فَتْحَة َ), answers "what was done?"

ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ عَمْرًا — Zayd struck ʿAmr زَيْدٌ = فَاعِل (ُ)  |  عَمْرًا = مَفْعُولٌ بِهِ (َ)

Arabic has 3 verb tenses / types:
الفِعْل الْمَاضِي — past tense (he did). Built on root letters, ends in ـَ for he-singular.
الفِعْل الْمُضَارِع — present/future (he does / he will do). Prefix يـ / تـ / أـ / نـ.
فِعْل الأَمْر — command (do!). Built from the مُضَارِع by removing the prefix and adjusting the vowel.

Demonstrative Pronouns (أَسْمَاءُ الإِشَارَةِ):

Near (this/these): هَذَا (m.s.) · هَذِهِ (f.s.) · هَذَانِ (m. dual) · هَاتَانِ (f. dual) · هَؤُلَاءِ (pl. humans)
Far (that/those): ذَلِكَ (m.s.) · تِلْكَ (f.s.) · ذَانِكَ (m. dual) · تَانِكَ (f. dual) · أُولَئِكَ (pl. humans)

Non-human plurals → use the feminine singular demonstrative: هَذِهِ الْكُتُبُ (these books).
